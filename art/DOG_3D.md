# Continuous 3D dog renderer

The normal home, run, and result dog is a single procedural, articulated WebGL model. It does not switch photo frames, mirror a pose, load a texture, or make a network request. Model and rig versions are `pom-coat-webgl-1.2.0` and `four-foot-contact-ik-1.2.0`.

The geometry uses 13,480 triangles, 8,245 vertices, 24 named bones, one vertex-color shader material, and no textures. Vertices carry smooth normals and are transformed by the bone matrices in the vertex shader. The local palette has 15 brown, cream, eye, nose, and mouth colors. Fixed bind bounds place the neutral paw plane at `y=0`; measured standing height is 1.355 model units. The model is built locally by `buildDog3DModel()` in `src/dog-3d-model.js`.

Coordinates use `x` across the dog, `y` up, and `z` toward its muzzle. `yaw: 0` faces the camera, `yaw: Math.PI / 2` faces screen right, and `yaw: Math.PI` faces away. The fixed standing root height is 0.555. A pose can lower or raise the root, but projection scale and paw reference do not change to fit that pose.

`sampleDog3DPose(args)` in `src/dog-3d-rig.js` is the deterministic sampler used by the renderer and by diagnostics. It accepts `timeMs` for continuous breathing, blinking, and ambient motion; `actionAgeMs` and `durationMs` for timed action phases; `groundDistance` and optional `velocity` for gait; `view` or `yaw`; and the supported actions below. It returns the 24 joint matrices, landmarks, four foot transforms, stride phase, stance state, foot lift, and world positions. `DOG_3D_RIG_DIAGNOSTICS` declares the coordinate system, bone IDs, stride metadata, named body landmarks, and contact method.

Walk and run phases derive from travelled distance, with stride lengths of 0.52 and 0.76 model units and stance ratios of 0.64 and 0.52. The four paws use two-bone IK. During stance, the same analytic spatial offset cancels root travel, including at different frame rates and speeds; swing follows a smooth quintic path and lifts the foot. The reported foot points come from the same paw matrices used by the vertex shader.

`Dog3DRenderer` in `src/dog-3d-renderer.js` lazily creates one transparent WebGL canvas on first draw. Its `draw(ctx, args)` placement arguments are `x`, `foot`, and `size`; pose arguments pass through to the sampler. Each pose uses one indexed triangle draw and the canvas is composited onto the existing Canvas2D scene. The offscreen canvas has a fixed 1.90×size height and 1.42×size foot baseline to leave room for the largest supported poses. Placement is not renormalized per action. Action changes blend joint transforms for 280 ms by default; turn yaw uses a smooth shortest-angle transition.

After a successful draw, `renderer.lastDraw.pose` contains the matrices uploaded for that frame. `lastDraw.landmarks` and `lastLandmarks` contain mouth, nose, eyes, crown, and tail tip in target-canvas CSS coordinates; `lastDraw.feet` exposes the four rendered paw points in the same coordinate space. `dog3DPointOnScreen(point, { x, foot, size, standingHeight })` is the shared projection helper. Controllers can call `resetPose(controllerId)` to clear animation and transition clocks without rebuilding GPU resources. A lost WebGL context returns `false` and records an error.

Supported actions are `idle`, `walk`, `run`, `turn`, `sit`, `lie`, `sleep`, `wake`, `stretch`, `yawn`, `headTilt`, `petHappy`, `bellyUp`, `happyJump`, `sleepyRefuse`, `lookPlayer`, `peek`, `lookFood`, `sitWait`, `sniffFood`, `eat`, `lick`, `playBow`, `retrieve`, `carry`, `return`, `tug`, `plushShake`, `plushCuddle`, `jumpCatch`, `enterHouse`, `insideHouse`, `exitHouse`, `rest`, `feed`, `sniff`, `cuddle`, `fall`, and `finish`.

Reduced Motion keeps breathing, blinking, spatial walking, and a slow tail while lowering body bounce, swing lift, head tilt, tail speed, and jump height. A paused renderer reuses the last complete pose per controller. `renderer.resetPose()` clears those cached poses when a clock restarts.

The model is a deterministic procedural interpretation of a fluffy brown-and-white Pomeranian: compact torso and limbs, broad coat silhouette, cream chest bib and forehead blaze, dark rounded eyes and warm dark nose, small upright ears, and curled plume tail. It is not a measured, photo-derived 3D scan. Exact likeness and naturalness remain a human review decision. The `humanDog` approval in `art/human-approvals.json` stays `PENDING`; geometry budgets, rig diagnostics, and automated screenshots do not grant that approval.

Focused unit checks run with `node --test tests/dog-3d*.test.mjs`. The browser harness should additionally inspect real transparent bounds, all supported poses and facings, world-space stance drift, attached-toy mouth coordinates, and transitions against the actual WebGL output.
